The AI-written draft of my frontend book claimed a third-party script tool isolates trackers from your form fields. Its own docs say it doesn't. I only caught it because I'd committed to checking every page in public.

I've just opened up Frontend Engineering as a Book of Choices, a free reference where all 20 pages have now been checked this way.

Most guides tell you which framework to use. This one starts from your constraints: does the page need to rank on Google, is the data personalised, does it update dozens of times a second, does it have to work offline? Then it shows the options that fit and what each one costs.

Inside:
• 9 chapters, from rendering and reactivity to delivery and local-first
• 5 decision playbooks for common product types
• A calculator: six questions in, a starting architecture with reasoning out, shareable as a link

How it's checked: AI writes the draft, then two separate AI runs check every claim against official docs (MDN, OWASP, web.dev, framework docs). The second run starts fresh, and it can only correct or delete claims, never add new ones. The claim-by-claim ledger is public.
Each page is labelled "AI-checked", and no human expert has reviewed any page yet.

If you work on frontends, be that human reviewer. Correct me, and send me your "we chose X, and this is what it cost us" in the comments or as a GitHub issue.
